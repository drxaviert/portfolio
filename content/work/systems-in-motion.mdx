## The concept

The brief was one sentence long, which turned out to be the useful constraint: make the first five minutes feel like the building is already open. Everything that did not answer it could go.

I was the design lead on the work. The first two weeks were reading: support threads, old decks, and the comments people leave when nobody is watching. The pattern was not that people wanted fewer choices. They wanted fewer moments where the product asked them to decide something it could have decided for them.

<Note n={1}>Six interviews, all with people who had used the service for at least a year.</Note>

## The system

We wrote the brief back as three lines and pinned it above the desk. If a screen needed a sentence to explain itself, the screen was not finished.

The motion rule took the same shape. One entrance curve, one duration scale, and an explicit list of places where nothing moves at all. The list was longer than the system, which was the point: a component library is mostly a record of what you chose not to add.

## The process

The first prototype was deliberately plain: one action per screen, one place for the thing you came to do, and a default that did not move. We tested it with six people who had never seen the old version. The notes were shorter than usual, which we took as a good sign.

Most of the work was subtraction. The last round removed a badge, a tour, two empty states and a small animation that had survived three reviews because nobody could remember who asked for it.

## The outcome

What stayed is not very photogenic: a written rule for every state, a decision table, and defaults that do not need to be explained. The release was quiet, which was the point.

<Note n={2}>The animation was 180ms. It survived two rounds before someone asked what it was for.</Note>

The best compliment came from someone who said they had stopped noticing the interface. The lesson was not about motion design. Restraint has to be designed and defended in the same way as any new surface, or it disappears the first time someone asks for one more badge.
