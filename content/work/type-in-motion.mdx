## The concept

The starting point was a complaint I kept hearing: the type was fine on a desk and wrong on a train. At seven in the morning, with the brightness down, the counters closed up and the punctuation disappeared.

The brief was to draw a text face for exactly that condition, and to decide up front where it would be allowed to move. Type that animates badly is worse than type that does not animate at all.

<Figure caption="The first pass, at reading size." />

## The system

Five weights, 812 glyphs, and one rule about spacing: the face had to hold its colour at 13px before it was allowed to look good at 48px.

Glyphs were drawn small and checked large, which is the reverse of the usual order and the reason the lowercase survives on a phone. The motion spec came after the drawing. It allows two moves, both opacity, both under 200ms: a line of text arriving, and a link resolving under the pointer.

<Note n={1}>
  Two moves, both opacity. No tracking animations, no per-letter entrances.
</Note>

<Brief>A face that reads at 13px first, and gets to look good later.</Brief>

## The process

Every round went out as a specimen on the oldest phone in the studio. We read it on the train, in a stairwell, and once in a basement with no signal. The notes were about comfort, not style, which made them easy to act on.

The last change was the hardest to see: the space between words went up by two units, and the paragraphs got quieter.

<Figure wide caption="The specimen, in the conditions it was drawn for." />

## The outcome

The face shipped in five weights with a variable axis for optical size. The motion rules shipped with it, in the same file, so the first person to animate a headline would find the limit before the idea.

<Note n={2}>
  Tested on a five-year-old phone at minimum brightness, which remains the only
  benchmark I trust.
</Note>

The quieter paragraph was the part readers noticed, though none of them could say why.
